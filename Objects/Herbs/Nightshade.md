The key ingredient in the assassin’s blood poison.

Description:
Nightshade is a slender, vining plant that thrives in dim and damp places — the roots often burrow into the shaded loam beneath stone walls or decaying logs. Its **leaves** are a deep, velvety green with a faint **purple-black sheen** when caught in torchlight, as though shadow pools in their veins. The **stems** are delicate but wiry, twisting in slow, deliberate spirals that cling to rough surfaces or neighboring plants.

The plant’s **flowers** bloom in small clusters of five-petaled bells, each petal fading from indigo at the base to a bruised violet at the tip. In bright light they appear dull, but under moonlight or fey glow they emit a faint iridescent shimmer — the visual cue most poisoners use to identify the true strain.

Its **berries** are small, glossy, and black as polished obsidian.  The calyx is broad and blunt.  When crushed, they release a thin, sweet scent of overripe fruit mixed with iron — deceptively pleasant and highly dangerous.

Look alike:
Mirror berry
Mirrorberry is a close cousin of Nightshade that mimics its form almost perfectly, though trained eyes can spot key differences. It shares the same wiry, vining growth and preference for shaded, damp soil, and its **leaves** are a lush, deep green with a faint **bluish-silver iridescence** rather than the **purple-black undertone** of true Nightshade. Under torchlight, the leaf veins catch the light and shimmer faintly — almost metallic — while Nightshade’s veins appear shadowed and dark.

The **flowers** are similar small, five-petaled bells in shades of violet, but where Nightshade’s petals fade from indigo to bruised purple, Mirrorberry’s fade from lavender to **soft blue at the edges**, giving them a cooler, paler hue overall. This difference is obvious in daylight, but nearly imperceptible in dim or colored light.

The **berries** are a glossy, deep maroon-black instead of pure black, with a faint **reddish glint** when turned in the light — a detail easily missed in poor conditions but unmistakable when viewed clearly. The calyx (the small star-shaped leaf crown at the base of each berry) is **narrower and sharply pointed**, where Nightshade’s is broader and blunter. When crushed, Mirrorberry smells faintly of **bitter almonds and ozone**, not fruit and iron.

- **Save:** DC 13 Constitution.
    
- **Fail:** The creature is **Poisoned** for 1 hour. During this time, they experience fleeting hallucinations — faint whispers, movement in shadows, distorted colors. Disadvantage on Perception and Insight checks.
